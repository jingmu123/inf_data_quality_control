【0】A pilot case–control study on the alveolar bone density measurement in risk assessment for bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw
The first case is a 75-year-old woman with multiple myeloma treated with 10 mg monthly intravenous incadronate for 5 years along with dexamethasone, ranimustine, vincristine, and interferon. In June 2006, right maxillary canine, right maxillary first premolar, and left mandibular first molar were extracted.
As shown in Fig. a dental X-ray film view revealed disappearance of the trabecular structure of the mandible. Pathological findings were characteristic of BRONJ with scarcely any osteocytes visible in the area involved; a radio-opaque area surrounded by relatively radiolucent area interspersed with bacterial flora and inflammatory granulation tissue, indicating chronic suppurative osteomyelitis. The bone mineral density was extremely high around the BRONJ lesion, 181.3 ± 5.0 (6, 7, 8, means ± SD, N = 3), far exceeding the mean bone mineral density in healthy young subjects and significantly higher than the density around the non-necrotic areas, 146.4 ± 19.1 (1, 2, 3, mean ± SD, N = 3) where no BRONJ occurred . Although right maxillary canine and first premolar as well as left mandibular first molar were also extracted along with the left mandibular molar, BRONJ developed only around the left first molar of the mandible. Detection and evaluation of locally high BMD in the jaw bone apparently made an early detection of BRONJ possible. Apparently, dental extraction and accompanying tissue damage, infection, hemorrhage, etc. accelerate or provoke infectious or necrotic process in the development of BRONJ.
