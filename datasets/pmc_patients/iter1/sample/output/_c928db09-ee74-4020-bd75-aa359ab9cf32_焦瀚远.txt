【0】Adenosquamous Carcinoma of the Ascending Colon: A Case Report and Review of the Literature

The result of microscopic examination was reported as a moderately-differentiated adenocarcinoma, and she came to our hospital for surgical treatment. She only had constipation and had not shown any other specific symptoms or signs related with the cancer. There was no significant past medical history and no family history of colorectal cancer. The patient's serum carcinoembryonic antigen level was 1.22 ng/mL, and her serum hemoglobin level was 10.9 g/dL. There was no significant abnormal result among initial laboratory findings. There was no evidence of distant metastasis on the positron emission tomographic scan.

Finally, the mass was diagnosed as an adenosquamous carcinoma of stage IIIB (T3 N2a M0) according to the 7th edition of the colon and rectum cancer staging system published by the American Joint Committee on Cancer.
